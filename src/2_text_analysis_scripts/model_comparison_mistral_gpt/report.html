LLM-Generated Swedish — Results Report

Human vs GPT-5.2 vs Mistral (mistral-small-2506), two registers × four prompt conditions. Paired Wilcoxon + Cohen's dz + BH-FDR. Effect-size-first — at this n, p-values alone are uninformative.

Key findings

RQ1 · Detectability is register-dependent

LLM Swedish diverges from human far more in formal abstracts (median |dz| 0.29–0.56, small–medium) than in informal comments (0.16–0.24, negligible–small), across both models. Short casual comments have little stylometric surface area; structured academic prose exposes the tells.

RQ2 · Adversarial prompting fails — and backfires

Prompting the model to 'sound human / evade detection' does not reduce its structural distance from human; 10 of 12 model×register×strategy cells are neutral or more detectable than baseline. The detector-aware strategy is the most counterproductive, via over-compliance (abnormally choppy prose).

RQ3 · Model identity matters most

GPT-5.2 and Mistral differ at medium-or-larger effect on 183/532 features (99 large). Mistral is the more human-like generator in formal Swedish (closer to human on 61% of formal features). Under adversarial prompts Mistral over-compresses (shorter, more function words/periods) while GPT stays long.

RQ4 · Affect catches the informal tell stylometry misses

Top-label sentiment is identical across sources (~77% neutral), but continuous signed polarity reveals a GPT positivity bias at baseline (dz +0.57 vs human; +0.74 vs Mistral — the largest single affective effect). Adversarial prompts move affect but overshoot (GPT swings from too-positive to more-negative-than-human); Mistral shows no positivity bias and tracks human throughout.
