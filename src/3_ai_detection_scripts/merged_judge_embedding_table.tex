  \caption{AI-text detection by generator, register and prompt condition, comparing
  three large-language-model judges with an embedding-based classifier.
  \emph{AI recall} is the share of generated items the judge flagged as AI;
  \emph{human precision} is the share of the items the judge labelled \emph{human}
  that really are human, pooled within each cell against the register's human items.
  \emph{Embedding CV ROC AUC} is a logistic-regression two-sample test on multilingual
  sentence embeddings (\texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2}, 5-fold CV);
  $0.5$ is chance and higher means more separable from human text. Bold marks the most
  detectable prompt condition within each generator and register block, by AUC.
  \textbf{The two halves of the table are not measured on the same items.} The judge
  columns score $n=100$ generated items per cell, whereas every AUC cell is computed on
  the matched $100$-document GPT-5.6 quiz subset so that generators stay comparable
  (a cross-validated AUC falls as $n$ falls; see \texttt{EMBEDDING\_AUC\_CAVEATS.md}).
  Read the two halves as parallel evidence, not as paired measurements.
  \emph{--} marks the Gemini formal cells, which were never scored:
  \texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview} is capped at 250 requests/day on the available key,
  which did not permit a 998-item run within the study period. Claude's formal cells
  are scored on $n=88$--$90$ rather than $100$; all six sit at $98$--$100\%$, so the
  remaining items cannot move them materially. The formal GPT-5.2 rows are scored on
  the original corpus while the formal GPT-5.6 and Mistral rows use the
  formatting-normalized corpus, so \textbf{the formal judge columns are not internally
  comparable}: on the normalized corpus DeepSeek scores $86\%$ pooled recall on the
