# Caveats for `embedding_auc_table.tex` (tab:embedding-auc)

Written 2026-08-02. Source of truth:
`src/2_text_analysis_scripts/csv_files/embedding_twosample_<register>_<condition>[_gpt56|_mistral_t1.0].csv`,
written by `2_text_analysis_scripts/scripts/embedding_comparison.py`.
The table itself is generated by `make_embedding_auc_table.py` — regenerate rather than hand-edit.

---

## 1. GPT-5.6 rests on a much smaller sample than the other two generators

The table places three generators side by side with no indication that they are
measured on very different numbers of documents.

| Generator | Formal n/class | Informal n/class |
|---|---|---|
| GPT-5.2 | 170 | **1,149** |
| Mistral 3.2 | 170 | **1,149** |
| GPT-5.6 | 100 (98 for detector-evasive) | **100** |

Informally that is an **≈11× difference in n**. GPT-5.6 was only ever generated for
a 100-item quiz subset (`*_gpt-5.6_quiz100.csv`), not the full corpus.

### Why it matters for how the table reads

The headline contrast a reader will draw is that GPT-5.6 is far harder to detect
than GPT-5.2 in the informal register (0.789 vs 0.960). That gap survives a
like-for-like comparison but shrinks by ~40% (see the matched control below), and
the *precision* behind the two numbers is not remotely comparable.
Approximate 95% CIs (Hanley–McNeil):

| Generator | Register | AUC | n/class | approx 95% CI | CI width |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-5.2 | informal | 0.960 | 1,149 | [0.952, 0.968] | 0.016 |
| Mistral 3.2 | informal | 0.965 | 1,149 | [0.957, 0.973] | 0.015 |
| **GPT-5.6** | **informal** | **0.789** | **100** | **[0.726, 0.852]** | **0.126** |
| GPT-5.2 | formal | 0.754 | 170 | [0.703, 0.805] | 0.103 |
| Mistral 3.2 | formal | 0.965 | 170 | [0.945, 0.985] | 0.040 |
| **GPT-5.6** | **formal** | **0.792** | **100** | **[0.729, 0.855]** | **0.125** |

The GPT-5.6 informal interval is **~8× wider** than GPT-5.2's. In the formal
register the published GPT-5.2 and GPT-5.6 intervals overlap heavily
([0.703, 0.805] vs [0.729, 0.855]), so the published formal pair cannot be
described as a difference — but note this is the *unmatched* comparison; once the
documents are matched the formal difference becomes large and clearly resolvable,
in the opposite direction to what the published pair suggests (see below).

These are normal-approximation intervals on a cross-validated AUC, so treat them
as indicative of relative precision, not as exact inference.

### The n disparity BIASES the comparison — it does not merely blur it

**This section corrects an earlier draft of this file, which claimed small n
"widens intervals but does not bias the estimate". That is true of a fixed
statistic, but false here.** The reported number is a *cross-validated classifier*
AUC: fewer documents means fewer training examples per fold, a weaker fitted
classifier, and therefore a **systematically lower** AUC. Measuring GPT-5.6 at
n=100 while GPT-5.2 and Mistral get n=170/1,149 handicaps GPT-5.6.

The document-matched control (below) confirms this: restricting the other two
generators to n=100 lowered **every single one of their 12 AUCs**, by 0.023 to
0.121. That is a one-directional effect, not noise.

### Document-matched control (run 2026-08-02)

Recomputed the GPT-5.2 and Mistral AUCs over exactly the documents the GPT-5.6
quiz used — same embedding model, same CV, same seed. Script:
`2_text_analysis_scripts/scripts/matched_auc_control.py`; results:
`2_text_analysis_scripts/csv_files/embedding_auc_matched_gpt56subset.csv`.
Validity check: recomputing GPT-5.6 this way reproduced its published AUCs to
four decimals (Δ = 0.0000 on all six cells), so the pipeline matches
`embedding_comparison.py`.

Baseline condition, all at n=100 per class:

| Generator | Register | Published | **Matched** | Δ | Matched 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-5.2 | formal | 0.754 (n=170) | **0.643** | −0.111 | [0.567, 0.719] |
| Mistral 3.2 | formal | 0.965 (n=170) | **0.935** | −0.030 | [0.899, 0.971] |
| GPT-5.6 | formal | 0.792 (n=100) | **0.792** | 0.000 | [0.729, 0.855] |
| GPT-5.2 | informal | 0.960 (n=1,149) | **0.886** | −0.074 | [0.839, 0.933] |
| Mistral 3.2 | informal | 0.965 (n=1,149) | **0.916** | −0.048 | [0.876, 0.957] |
| GPT-5.6 | informal | 0.789 (n=100) | **0.789** | 0.000 | [0.727, 0.852] |

**What this changes:**

1. **Formal: the published table gets the GPT-5.2 vs GPT-5.6 ordering backwards.**
   Published, GPT-5.2 (0.754) and GPT-5.6 (0.792) look near-identical, and an
   earlier draft of this file called the difference unresolvable. Matched, it is
   **0.643 vs 0.792 with non-overlapping CIs** — GPT-5.6 formal text is
   *substantially more* detectable than GPT-5.2's, not comparable to it. Any
   claim resting on the published formal pair should be rewritten.

2. **Informal: the headline gap is real but ~40% overstated.** Published, GPT-5.2
   0.960 vs GPT-5.6 0.789 = 0.171. Matched, 0.886 vs 0.789 = **0.097**. GPT-5.6
   informal text is still the harder to detect, but by roughly half of what the
   table implies.

3. **Mistral remains the most detectable generator in both registers**, matched
   or not. That conclusion is robust.

Note the formal GPT-5.2 delta (−0.111) exceeds the informal one (−0.074) despite
a far smaller reduction in n (170→100 vs 1,149→100), so **sample composition
matters as well as sample size** — which 100 documents were drawn is doing real
work, independently of how many.

### Minimum fix if the table ships as-is

Add an `n` column, or state the per-generator n in the caption. A reader cannot
currently tell that one row block is built on 100 documents and the others on
1,149 — nor that this depresses the 100-document row. **Preferably, report the
matched figures for cross-generator comparison** and keep the full-corpus numbers
only for within-generator, across-condition contrasts, where n is constant.

## 2. `\caption{CAPTION}` is a literal placeholder

`make_embedding_auc_table.py` emits the string `CAPTION` verbatim. It will
typeset as the word "CAPTION". The caption has to be written by hand — the
generator does not produce one. Whatever is written should carry the n
disclosure from §1.

## 3. detector-evasive formal GPT-5.6 is n=98, not 100

Two documents are missing from that one cell. Minor, but it means the GPT-5.6
formal block is not internally constant in n.

## 4. detector_aware is absent by design

Excluded from all reported results, and the GPT-5.6 quiz corpus was never
generated under it. Not an omission to fix.
